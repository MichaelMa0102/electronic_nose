# Orange Peel Trial 002

## Purpose

Second independent orange-peel response trial for the ESP32-S3 + BME688 electronic-nose prototype.

This trial is especially useful because ambient humidity differs substantially from Trial 001, giving an early look at cross-condition repeatability.

## Hardware

Same current setup as Trial 001:

- ESP32-S3-N16R8
- BME688 breakout
- I2C address: `0x77`
- SDA: GPIO 8
- SCL: GPIO 9
- Supply: 3.3 V

## Event timing and uncertainty

The physical events were not electronically marked during this run.

User record:
- Orange peel brought near the sensor at approximately **120,000 ms**, but this time is uncertain.
- Orange peel removed at approximately **230,000 ms**.

Sensor evidence:
- Gas resistance is stable near 410–413 kOhm immediately before the event.
- The first large discontinuity appears at **111,258 ms**.

To avoid inventing precision, the processed file uses:
- `baseline`: `< 111,258 ms`
- `exposure_transition`: `111,258–120,000 ms`
- `exposure`: `120,000–230,000 ms`
- `recovery`: `>= 230,000 ms`

For baseline statistics, the stable **60–110 s** interval is used.

## Trial 002 quantitative results

- Samples: **389**
- Stable baseline gas resistance: **411.5 kOhm**
- Baseline gas-resistance SD: **1.11 kOhm**
- Baseline CV: **0.270%**
- Minimum gas resistance during exposure: **160.2 kOhm** at **163.3 s**
- Maximum decrease from stable baseline: **61.1%**
- Stable-baseline humidity: **51.00% RH**
- Peak exposure humidity: **55.90% RH** at **177.0 s**
- Humidity increase: **4.90 percentage points**
- Mean gas resistance over final 60 s: **386.5 kOhm**
- Approximate recovery toward the original baseline by the end: **90.0%**

## Interpretation

### 1. The sensor response is clearly real relative to baseline noise

Trial 002 baseline CV is only **0.270%**, while the orange-peel exposure produces a **61.1%** drop in gas resistance. The response is therefore much larger than ordinary short-term baseline fluctuation.

### 2. A second independent trial again produces a large reversible response

Both Trial 001 and Trial 002 show a large fall in gas resistance after orange-peel exposure followed by recovery toward roughly 388 kOhm.

The exact response magnitude differs:
- Trial 001: **51.3%** drop
- Trial 002: **61.1%** drop

This is encouraging evidence of repeatability, but two trials are not enough for a robust statistical claim.

### 3. Humidity is a major confound and must not be ignored

The two trials start at very different ambient humidity:
- Trial 001: about **58.97% RH**
- Trial 002: about **51.00% RH**

Humidity also rises during orange exposure in both trials. Therefore the current signal cannot yet be interpreted as an orange-specific VOC fingerprint. A classifier could potentially exploit humidity rather than odor chemistry.

### 4. Current hardware is informative enough to justify continued controlled testing

The present bottleneck is no longer "does the sensor detect anything?" It clearly does. The next question is whether response features distinguish odors after controlling for humidity, trial timing, distance, and day-to-day drift.

## Next action

Before training a classifier:
1. Add electronic event markers to the logger.
2. Standardize sample amount and sensor distance.
3. Collect multiple independent orange trials.
4. Add one contrasting odor under the same protocol.
5. Evaluate full-trial generalization rather than randomly splitting time samples.
